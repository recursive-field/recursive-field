## Inheritance Finding

The dependency trace shows that “inheritance” is not one mechanism:

| Mode | Current expression | What survives | What can silently change |

| Shadowing | Earlier project/profile/user/plugin root wins a same-named resource | A deterministic current winner | Historical source, shadowed alternatives, and trust |
| Overlay | Profile settings or metadata dictionaries merge in order | Selected fields and accumulated origin categories | Exact prior values, omission history, and compatibility |
| History-borne retention | Loaded skill body remains in persisted chat history | Prior supplied instruction text and limited metadata | Current source under the same name and later applicability |

| Provider continuation | Local metadata retains model-affine provider response IDs | A continuation coordinate when the provider object remains available | Provider custody, transport semantics, model affinity, and remote retention |

**Target Requirement:** future inheritance must name its mode, source and destination scopes, conflict rule, revocation behavior, authority effect, provenance, and recovery consequence. No inherited relation grants authority merely by surviving.

## Selection, Enforcement, Authority, and Consequence

Chapter 2 can now preserve four distinct layers:

1. **Selection** chooses a candidate input, implementation, mode, or policy through precedence or configuration.
2. **Projection** presents some selected material to a model, participant, API, or observation surface.
3. **Enforcement** structurally permits, blocks, or constrains an operation at a boundary that the operation must cross.
4. **Authority** establishes who may decide and under what scope; it is not created by selection, visibility, possession, reachability, or successful execution.

A fifth relation follows actuation: **consequence verification** checks the effective downstream state and whether reach, authority, blast radius, persistence, or recovery changed. It cannot be replaced by a local success signal.
